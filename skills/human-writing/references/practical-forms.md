# Practical Forms

Load only for the requested form below. Use the supplied brief and authoritative
sources; the reader-task choices here do not create facts, commitments, recipients,
or permission to send. Apply only the matching profile, not every subsection.
These profiles inherit the fact-integrity rules and the operation boundaries in
content-modes; proofreading or a local edit does not authorize restructuring.

## Message Or Email

Identify whether the recipient needs information, a decision, a reply, or an action.
Put that purpose where it can be found; a subject line, greeting, signature, or next
step is optional unless the brief requires one. Preserve relationship-specific
courtesy and formality rather than forcing either corporate warmth or casualness.

- Distinguish the sender's request, offer, preference, and confirmed commitment.
  A proposed time is not an appointment; a budget ceiling is not an agreed price.
- Make a required reply, deadline, owner, or dependency explicit when supplied.
  Do not invent a deadline or make the recipient responsible for an unresolved task.
- Respect the allowed deletion scope. Permission to reorganize or remove repetition
  does not authorize dropping unique supplied facts. In coordination changes, keep
  the original arrangement distinct from current status and the proposed alternatives;
  a future option does not communicate what was previously agreed or planned.
  Select away unique details only when the brief permits selection or a summary.
- Use genuine correspondence samples, if available within the task, for relational
  tone. Absence of samples permits a restrained draft; it does not require inbox
  access, invented familiarity, or a fabricated shared history.

## Status Update Or Notice

Choose the information the recipient needs to decide, coordinate, or comply with.
A progress update can distinguish completed work, intended work, and blockers; a
notice can lead with the affected people, change, time, conditions, and action.
These are selection criteria, not compulsory headings.

Keep the reporting window and evidence strength attached to the status. A trial,
review, passed check, rollout, and acceptance are different events. Preserve named
owners and dependencies when they determine the next action. Avoid converting an
unassigned concern into an obligation or making every update sound successful.

For an event or operational notice, check that compression leaves participation
conditions and relevant preparation or safety boundaries clear. Select facts by the
recipient's role: a first-time participant may need prerequisites that an already
registered reader does not. Retain explicit exclusions and negative conditions.

## Product Description Or Release Note

Separate a product description's fit and mechanism from a release note's change and
its effect on existing users. Use only the supplied current behavior, evidence,
version, and release status. An announcement does not establish availability.

For an upgrade decision, prioritize any supplied compatibility break, changed
behavior, action, migration step, or limitation before secondary implementation
history. A changelog entry can stay short when it names an observable user change;
do not invent a benefit, metric, customer problem, or migration instruction to fill
an expected format. Keep source interest and testing boundaries visible when they
matter to interpretation.

## Analysis Or Commentary

Frame the question or position the reader should assess, then distinguish source
observations, the author's inference, and the recommendation or judgment. An opinion
may be confident without pretending to be a measured finding. Preserve the author's supplied stance, if any;
do not turn every conclusion into an interchangeable cautious summary.

Use comparisons or counterarguments when they test the claim, and preserve their
scope and strongest relevant evidence. Do not invent an opponent, reader misconception,
or causal mechanism. If the sources cannot resolve a question, state the remaining
uncertainty rather than manufacture a balanced verdict. Tables, evidence chains,
chronology, and narrative are options selected by the question, not mandatory forms.
